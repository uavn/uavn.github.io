uavn.github.io
==============

GitHub Page

## Build

The site is assembled into `_site` with:

```sh
scripts/build-site.sh
```

External apps are not committed in this repository. During the build,
`scripts/fetch-external-app.sh` reads `scripts/external-apps.conf`, fetches the
latest configured branches, and places each app under `_site/<app>`.

Local and CI builds use a token by default. For local builds, create
`.env.local`:

```sh
EXTERNAL_APPS_TOKEN=...
```

Then run:

```sh
scripts/build-site.sh
```

If `.env.local` is absent, the script also accepts `EXTERNAL_APPS_TOKEN`,
`GH_TOKEN`, or `GITHUB_TOKEN` from the current shell, and will try
`gh auth token` when the GitHub CLI is logged in.

In GitHub Actions, add a repository secret named `EXTERNAL_APPS_TOKEN` with
read access to the external app repositories. CI uses that token automatically.

External app routes currently generated by the build:

- `musvis` from `uavn/audio-visualizer`
- `mapvis` from `uavn/mapvis`
- `movanova` from `uavn/nova-mova`
- `unicorn-noir` from `uavn/unicorn-noir`

## Games in this repository

Anything under `games/<name>/` with a `package.json` is built during
`scripts/build-site.sh` (`npm ci && npm run build`) and published at `/<name>`.
The build output and `node_modules` stay out of git.

- `games/medieval-drift` → `/medieval-drift`

## Photos

The Photos app (`/photos`) shows the film photos from
[Lomography](https://www.lomography.com/homes/artbonvic). Lomography is behind a
Cloudflare challenge and has no CORS, so the page can't read it live; instead it
reads `photos/lomography.json`, while the images themselves load straight from
Lomography's CDN.

To refresh the list, open https://www.lomography.com/homes/artbonvic/photos in a
browser, paste `scripts/lomography-sync.js` into the DevTools console, and save
the downloaded `lomography.json` over `photos/lomography.json`.

## Photo Reel

`/photo-reel` turns a set of photos into a slideshow video: each photo sits on a
coloured (or blurred) background with an optional frame — border, rough edge,
filed negative carrier, 35mm strip, 120 film, Polaroid or Instax. Everything runs
in the browser: frames are drawn on a canvas, encoded with WebCodecs (H.264, up
to 4K/60) and muxed to MP4 with `mp4-muxer`. In Chrome the file streams straight
to disk; elsewhere it's built in memory and downloaded.

Camera sounds in `photo-reel/sounds/` are cut from CC0 recordings on
Freesound: the wind lever from
["analog camera wind and shutter click #2"](https://freesound.org/s/170139/) by
jgrzinich, the film shutter from
["Camera shutter click"](https://freesound.org/s/554268/) by Clagnar, and the
digital shutter from
["camera DSLR single shots"](https://freesound.org/s/453782/) by kyles.

## Film Dev

`/film-dev` is a film development timer. Pick a process (Kodak Flexicolor
C-41, CineStill Cs41, Tetenal E-6, a few B&W developers, or your
own), set box speed and the EI you shot at, and it works out the developer
time for push/pull, developer temperature and how many rolls the chemistry has
already done. Then it talks you through every step — pour, agitate, invert,
drain, get the next bath ready — with a pause between steps that waits for a
click or counts down. Processes are editable and saved in the browser; a run
survives a page reload. The preset times are starting points: check your
kit's sheet.

Push/pull uses the makers' own tables where they exist: Kodak Z-131 for
Flexicolor C-41 (+30 s a stop), Kodak F-4017 for Tri-X in D-76 and HC-110,
Ilford's HP5 Plus sheet for ID-11, Tetenal's sheet for E-6 and CineStill's
chart for Cs41. Stops beyond a table carry on from its last entry. Developer
temperature uses the makers' time/temperature tables where they exist (Kodak
F-4017, CineStill) and otherwise a percentage per degree (≈10% for B&W, as in
Ilford's chart; ≈15% for C-41, which Kodak doesn't tabulate).

The illustrations in `film-dev/art/` were generated with Gemini and cut into
transparent WebP files.

## Thumbnail

`/thumbnail` makes YouTube thumbnails (16:9, exported at 1280×720, 1080p or
4K; JPEG is kept under YouTube's 2 MB). A thumbnail is a stack of layers you
drag, resize and rotate on the canvas:

- **Photos** with film looks (presets, colour sliders, halation) and your own
  `.cube` LUTs.
- **Text** with style presets and drawn backgrounds: brush, marker, masking
  tape, torn paper, sticker, film strip, label tape, stamp, speech bubble,
  burst and more. The backgrounds are drawn in code, so any colour works.
- **Stickers**: emoji and Bootstrap Icons in themed groups (recolourable, with
  a die-cut edge or a badge), hand-drawn graphics, the Film Dev illustrations
  and sticker pictures (meme cats, moto and travel, cars, groceries and money)
  in `thumbnail/stickers/`, generated with Gemini and cut into transparent
  WebP files.
- **Drawing tools**: Arrow, Circle and Box. Drag on the canvas to draw them.
- **Clip art**: 54 flat retro-style illustrations in `thumbnail/clipart/` (travel,
  cars and moto, bikes, food, home, everyday things, shopping, nature, film
  photography), generated with Gemini and cut into transparent WebP files.
- **Flags**: Ukraine, the EU, Canada and every Canadian province and territory,
  as SVG files from Wikimedia Commons in `thumbnail/flags/`.
- **Finish** on top: one effect with its own settings — film (grain, light
  leaks, dust), old photo, VHS, CRT TV (PAL/NTSC scanlines), night vision,
  CCTV, thermal camera, 8-bit, comic halftone or glitch. It sits just above the
  top photo, so text stays clean unless you choose otherwise.

A YouTube-style feed preview (dark, light and sidebar sizes) shows how the
thumbnail reads when small. The project, its photos and LUTs are kept in the
browser's IndexedDB, with undo and redo.
